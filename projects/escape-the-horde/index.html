Escape the Horde

An asymmetric survival game where survivors explore and escape while an opponent commands an infected force. I connect the rules, spaces and multiplayer systems with custom real-time shading, lighting and character animation.

Different roles, different decisions

Survivors and the infected commander share a manor but have different information, resources and kinds of control. My work connects escape objectives, spatial exploration, sound, squad commands and direct possession into an asymmetric contest.

Information is part of the level

Exploration reveals useful space and routes while sound can give the opposing side a clue. In the current development version, unexplored map areas stay hidden and sound cues do not directly reveal a survivor’s identity. The level shapes what each side can know and how confidently it can act.

AI that respects player intent

Commanded units must hold when told to hold, rather than abandon a plan as soon as they notice an opponent. Survivor teammates balance escorting, assigned tasks, limited ammunition and the risk of opening fire. These rules support player control and understandable consequences.

Design and implementation

I work across the rules and their implementation in TypeScript, Three.js and Node/WebSocket multiplayer, including shared state, AI, scene integration, interface and feedback. The project is a working prototype undergoing iteration and testing.

Building the visual world

In the evolving local build, I integrate 3D models, materials and skeletal animation with custom surface shading and depth-based contours. Scene-aware lighting accounts for rooms, floors, occlusion, flashlights and power states, connecting the visual treatment to the space and its gameplay.

Choosing how characters are rendered

For distant crowds, I bake character poses into position and normal textures and interpolate them in an instanced rendering path. Nearby and action-focused characters retain skeletal animation. This is an implemented rendering approach; I do not claim a measured performance improvement without a controlled comparison.

A rendering study built around the game

A dedicated local study environment compares visual treatments on the game’s assets, including ink-like shading, outlines and stop-motion poses. It also renders multi-direction sprite atlases from animated models. This lets me explore visual identity through working implementations while keeping the gameplay project as the context.

Version and development status

The earlier Escape the Horde prototype supported 2–3 players. Current local development, under the name DEAD AIR, explores a 1v4 format with AI filling empty roles. The images and public demo on this page are retained as earlier work; full remote multiplayer validation for the current version remains ongoing.
